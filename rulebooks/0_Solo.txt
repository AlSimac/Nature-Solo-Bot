INTRODUCTION
In Nature Solo, you play against an automated opponent named Rani (“rah-nee”) who uses a deck of solo
cards to make decisions. Nature Solo can be played at five different difficulty levels.
A game of Nature Solo is played using the standard Nature rules. Additionally, Rani has special rules that will
take precedence if there is a conflict. Can you adapt better than Rani?

SETUP
Nature Solo is set up like a 2-player game of Nature. Follow the steps on page 4 of the Nature rulebook then
do the following:
• Choose a difficulty: Mouse (easiest), Rabbit, Fox (medium), Tiger, or Snow Leopard (most
challenging). If it is your first game, we recommend playing at the Mouse difficulty.
• Set up a player screen for Rani.
• Shuffle and place the six solo cards face-down to create Rani’s deck.
• Choose who will play first between you and Rani. There will be four rounds.
PREPARATION
New Species
When getting a new species for Rani use the standard rules (Nature, page 7) with the following changes:
• When Rani gets a new species, it is always placed to the right of any existing species in her player area.
• Each of Rani’s species will have a solo card. Draw the top card of Rani’s deck and play it face-up with
the new species. This is not a trait card, so it does not count toward the limit of 3 traits.
• Whenever drawing a new solo card, cover the icons below the blue tree (the right-hand side) using the
size dial, or any component you prefer. Pay attention to any species instructions, if applicable.
First Round Only
Rani starts the game with an additional species. After creating Rani’s first species, immediately make a
second species for Rani. Draw a solo card for each species.
The second species starts with population equal to the number in your selected difficulty:
Draw Cards
Rani draws cards equal to the number in your selected difficulty:
Do not look at Rani’s cards. Place them face-down in a stack, hereafter called her “hand.” If Rani has any
cards set aside from a previous round, shuffle those into her hand after drawing new cards.
ADAPTING PHASE
You always go first during the Adapting Phase.
The Adapting Phase uses the standard rules (Nature, page 8) with the following changes for Rani:
Fourth Round Only
Set aside one card from Rani’s hand for each of Rani’s species that can forage from the watering hole. Keep

back in her hand.
Play Rani’s Hand
Draw a card from the top of Rani’s hand one at a time. Follow these steps for
each card drawn:
1. Check the solo icon in the bottom left corner of the drawn card to
determine species priority.
Each solo card has four columns of solo icons organized into four
priority lists. These lists are read from top to bottom when determining
species priority. The priority list is divided into sides: “purple tree”(left)
and “blue tree”(right).
Each side has two columns divided by the round; “round 1-3” and
“round 4”. To determine which species has priority, use the visible
priority list corresponding to the correct round. Note: Rani can switch
sides during the game.
2. Assign this card to Rani’s species with the solo icon highest on its priority list. Whenever two or
more species have the solo icon at the same priority, assign it to the leftmost (oldest) species first.

RANI’S FEEDING PHASE
The Feeding Phase uses the standard rules (Nature, page 14) with the following changes for Rani:
1. Rani will always forage first if she can. Rani will first eat with the species that will provide her with the
most food by foraging. If two or more are tied, she will eat with the leftmost (oldest) species.
2. If Rani cannot take the forage action, she will hunt, starting with the leftmost (oldest) species that can hunt.
Foraging Species
Rani will choose which forager to eat based on plant available in the watering hole.
When determining how much food can be eaten, Rani will consider there to be three additional plants
in the watering hole for each card left in her hand. For example, if Rani has two cards in her hand, with one
plant remaining in the watering hole, she will consider there to be seven plants in the watering hole,
six from the two cards she has in her hand plus one visible in the watering hole.
Before the forager eats, if there is less food in the watering hole than the forager can eat, Rani will discard a
random card from her hand and add plants to the watering hole equal to the food value printed on the
discarded card. She will do this multiple times if she is able until there is as much plants as the forager
can eat.

Hunting Species
Rani will choose which species to feed when hunting, in this order:
1. She will eat the species that loses her the least population across all her species.
(For example: Rani can eat 2 species. One is your size 4 species with Horned which will kill 4 of Rani’s
species population . The other is her own which will only kill 1 population on her hunted species.
She chooses to eat her own species and lose 1 population instead of 4.)
2. She will prefer to eat one of your species, before her own.
3. If there are ties, she will try to eat the smallest (size dial only) of those species first.
4. If still tied, she will try to eat the leftmost (oldest) of those species first.
Starving
If Rani has hungry population but cannot eat, Rani will starve population in this order:
1. The leftmost (oldest) hunter.
2. Any forager that has not yet eaten; if two or more have not yet eaten, starve the one with the lowest
population first; if still tied, starve the leftmost (oldest) species.
3. Any remaining forager, starving leftmost (oldest) first, if more than one remains.
Losing Population
Rani will always choose a hungry population to be killed, in preference to a fed population . Keep any
killed population next to the solo card to keep track during the round.

Extinction
When one of Rani’s species goes extinct, place the solo card on the bottom of Rani’s deck and follow
extinction rules (Nature, page 17).
SCORING
Before scoring, place any of Rani’s killed population next to her player screen. For each of Rani’s species
that had 1 or more population killed this round:
• Switch which priority lists that species uses in the next round. If the species used the priority lists
below the blue tree in the last round, cover the priority lists below the blue tree and use the priority
lists below the purple tree next round and vice versa.
• Shuffle that species’ existing trait cards and discard one at random. If that species’ Hunter trait is
discarded, they will switch back to being a forager.
The game ends after four rounds. Score more points than Rani to win the game!
