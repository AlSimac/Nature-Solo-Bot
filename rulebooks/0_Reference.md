# Nature Reference Guide

The Nature reference guide provides additional detail on key words, trait cards, round structure, and iconography.

## Key Words

The key words have specific in-game meanings that explain how a trait will interact with the core rules and with other traits.

### Feeding Action

There are two basic feeding actions: foraging and hunting.

- Forage: when a species forages, it eats plant(s) from the watering hole equal to the number on its size dial.
- Hunt: when a species hunts, it eats meat from the supply equal to the size of the hunted species, and 1 population of the hunted species is killed.
- Starve: when a species starves, choose a hungry population of that species to be killed.
- Pass: once a player passes as their feeding action, they are out of the Feeding Phase for the remainder of the round.

### Other Actions

- Eats: a species may eat only if it has hungry population. When a species eats, place each eaten food on one of its hungry population.
- Killed: when a population is killed, flip it over so this side is showing and return any food on it to the supply. Killed population will be added as population to your new species in the next round. The player who controls the population that is killed chooses which population is killed.

### Other Key Words

- Card: a card is either in the draw deck, the discard pile, or your hand. Cards played on species are called traits. Discarding a card from your hand provides a benefit. Discarding a trait does not provide a benefit.
- Extinction: a species goes extinct when its last population is killed or starves.
- Food: plant and meat are food used to keep population from starving.
- Fed Population: a fed population has food on it.
- Fully Fed: a species is fully fed when none of its population are hungry.
- Hungry: a population is hungry when it doesn’t have food on it. A species is hungry when it has at least 1 hungry population.
- Ignores: a species that ignores a defensive trait while hunting acts as if that trait does not exist. This keyword is used in several modules.
- Size: every species has a size dial to indicate its size. The size of a species determines how effective it is at hunting, defending, and foraging.
- Species: a species consists of a size dial, at least 1 population, and between 0 and 3 traits.
- Trait: when a card is played on a species, it is called a trait. Each species may have up to 3 traits. Any trait that is removed from a species becomes a card again.

Each trait in the Nature deck has effects that are additive, so having two copies of the same trait will have double the effect, and having three copies will have triple the effect.

## Trait Cards

### Clawed

A species with Clawed adds 2 to their hunt value when hunting, and 1 to their defense value when being hunted.

When eating plants, a species with Clawed will eat 2 additional plants from the same source.

### Fast

A species with Fast cannot be hunted by a species with fewer copies of the Fast trait.

For example, a hunter with 1 copy of Fast can hunt a species with 1 copy of Fast, but cannot hunt a species with 2 or 3 copies of Fast.

### Horned

When a species with Horned is hunted, it will kill population of the hunter. The number of population it kills will be equal to its size.

For example, when a size 1 species with Horned is hunted, it will kill 1 population of the hunter, and when a size 3 species with Horned is hunted, it will kill 3 population of the hunter.

When a species with Horned is hunted, 1 of its population will be killed as normal, even if the hunter goes extinct.

### Hunter

When the Hunter trait is played, flip its size dial to the HUNTER side. A species with Hunter can no longer take the forage feeding action. It takes the hunt feeding action instead.

A hunter can hunt a species if:

- its hunt value is equal to or greater than the defense value of the hunted species,
- it has the traits necessary to overcome the hunted species’ defensive traits,
- it has hungry population.

### Opportunistic

A species with Opportunistic adds 1 to their defense value when being hunted.

Opportunistic triggers after any of your species forages or hunts, as long as there are plants in the watering hole after the feeding action is taken. When Opportunistic triggers, the species with Opportunistic eats 2 meat from the supply.

Each Opportunistic triggers independently. For example, if a species has 2 copies of Opportunistic and a Social, it would eat a total of 8 meat.

### Plated

A species with Plated adds 4 to their defense value when being hunted.

### Scavenging

Scavenging triggers after any species hunts. This may happen on your feeding turn or on someone else’s feeding turn.

When Scavenging triggers, the species with Scavenging eats 2 meat from the supply.

Each Scavenging triggers independently. For example, if a species has 2 copies of Scavenging and a Social, it would eat a total of 8 meat.

### Social

A species with Social gets 2 additional food when it eats. The additional food comes from the same source as the original food: from the watering hole when it’s plant(s), and from the supply when it’s meat.

Social works when a species forages or hunts, but it can be combined with any trait that eats, such as Opportunistic, Scavenging, and Tusked.

### Tusked

A species with Tusked adds 2 to their defense value when being hunted.

Tusked triggers any time the last plant is removed from the watering hole. Tusked traits trigger regardless of who the current player is and may trigger multiple times in a round if additional food is added to the watering hole.

When Tusked triggers, the species with Tusked eats 1 meat from the supply.

Each Tusked triggers independently. For example, if a species has 2 copies of Tusked and a Social, it would eat a total of 6 meat.

## Round Structure

This chart shows the round structure in Nature combined with additional steps from Modules 1-6.

### Preparation

1. Roll the climate die
2. Add plants to the watering hole
3. Add fed population to the faraway place
4. Get a new species
5. Draw cards

### Adapting Phase

1. Play cards
2. Reveal trait cards

### Feeding Phase

1. Move Fat Tissue food
2. Take feeding turns
3. Resolve climate

### Scoring

1. Migrated species return
2. Nesting triggers
3. Put food behind player screens
4. Game End Bonus
5. Flip Disaster Card

## Summary

The Reference Guide is a compact rules reference for the terminology, effects, round flow, and iconography used throughout Nature. It is intended to complement the full core rulebook rather than replace it.
