# Nature Solo Rulebook

## Introduction

In Nature Solo, you play against an automated opponent named Rani ("rah-nee") who uses a deck of solo cards to make decisions. Nature Solo can be played at five different difficulty levels.

A game of Nature Solo is played using the standard Nature rules. Additionally, Rani has special rules that take precedence if there is a conflict. Can you adapt better than Rani?

## Components

### Solo Cards

Each solo card includes:

- a species name
- instructions
- a set icon
- a solo icon

The solo card system defines how Rani chooses which species to assign cards to, when to grow population, when to increase size, and how to behave during feeding and starvation.

## Setup

Nature Solo is set up like a 2-player game of Nature. Follow the steps in the Nature rulebook, then do the following:

- Choose a difficulty: Mouse (easiest), Rabbit, Fox (medium), Tiger, or Snow Leopard (most challenging). If it is your first game, we recommend playing at the Mouse difficulty.
- Set up a player screen for Rani.
- Shuffle and place the six solo cards face-down to create Rani’s deck.
- Choose who will play first between you and Rani. There will be four rounds.

## Preparation

### New Species

When getting a new species for Rani, use the standard rules with the following changes:

- When Rani gets a new species, it is always placed to the right of any existing species in her player area.
- Each of Rani’s species will have a solo card. Draw the top card of Rani’s deck and play it face-up with the new species. This is not a trait card, so it does not count toward the limit of 3 traits.
- Whenever drawing a new solo card, cover the icons below the blue tree (the right-hand side) using the size dial, or any component you prefer. Pay attention to any species instructions, if applicable.

### First Round Only

Rani starts the game with an additional species. After creating Rani’s first species, immediately make a second species for Rani. Draw a solo card for each species.

The second species starts with population equal to the number in your selected difficulty.

### Draw Cards

Rani draws cards equal to the number in your selected difficulty.

Do not look at Rani’s cards. Place them face-down in a stack, hereafter called her “hand.” If Rani has any cards set aside from a previous round, shuffle those into her hand after drawing new cards.

## Adapting Phase

You always go first during the Adapting Phase.

The Adapting Phase uses the standard rules with the following changes for Rani:

### Fourth Round Only

Set aside one card from Rani’s hand for each of Rani’s species that can forage from the watering hole. Keep these cards face-down and set aside until step 3, when they are considered back in her hand.

### Play Rani’s Hand

Draw a card from the top of Rani’s hand one at a time. Follow these steps for each card drawn:

1. Check the solo icon in the bottom-left corner of the drawn card to determine species priority.
2. Assign this card to Rani’s species with the solo icon highest on its priority list. Whenever two or more species have the solo icon at the same priority, assign it to the leftmost (oldest) species first.
3. Each solo icon on the priority list has three unique presentations that determine the action taken:
   - No border: the card is added to the species as a trait.
   - In a circle: discard the card and increase population.
   - In a D-shaped border: discard the card and increase size.
   - If the species is already at maximum size, skip this species and assign the drawn card to the species with this solo icon in the next highest priority.
4. The solo icon may also be red, which requires an additional step:
   - Red Icon: Give this species the Hunter trait if it is not a hunter.
   - If this species already has three traits, shuffle its existing trait cards and randomly discard one before adding the Hunter trait.
5. If no species can be assigned the card, Rani will keep the card face-down in her hand.

### Special Rule: No Foraging Species

If Rani has no species that can forage from the watering hole in the next phase, then follow the additional Adapting Phase rules below:

- Rani will use all cards in her face-down hand from step 2 to increase size for her species. For each card, Rani will increase the size of the species that has the card’s solo icon at the highest priority.
- If that species is at maximum size, move on to the next species with the card’s solo icon at the highest priority.
- If priority is tied, the card goes to the leftmost (oldest) species first.
- Once all of Rani’s species are at maximum size, she will use any remaining cards to increase population for the species with the card’s solo icon at the highest priority.
- If priority is tied, the card goes to the leftmost (oldest) species first.

## Rani’s Feeding Phase

The Feeding Phase uses the standard rules with the following changes for Rani:

1. Rani will always forage first if she can. Rani will first eat with the species that will provide her with the most food by foraging. If two or more are tied, she will eat with the leftmost (oldest) species.
2. If Rani cannot take the forage action, she will hunt, starting with the leftmost (oldest) species that can hunt.

### Foraging Species

Rani will choose which forager to eat based on plant availability in the watering hole.

When determining how much food can be eaten, Rani will consider there to be three additional plants in the watering hole for each card left in her hand. For example, if Rani has two cards in her hand and one plant remaining in the watering hole, she will consider there to be seven plants in the watering hole: six from the two cards she has in her hand plus one visible in the watering hole.

Before the forager eats, if there is less food in the watering hole than the forager can eat, Rani will discard a random card from her hand and add plants to the watering hole equal to the food value printed on the discarded card. She will do this multiple times if needed until there is as much plant as the forager can eat.

### Hunting Species

Rani will choose which species to feed when hunting, in this order:

1. She will eat the species that loses her the least population across all her species.
2. She will prefer to eat one of your species before her own.
3. If there are ties, she will try to eat the smallest (size dial only) of those species first.
4. If still tied, she will try to eat the leftmost (oldest) of those species first.

### Starving

If Rani has hungry population but cannot eat, Rani will starve population in this order:

1. The leftmost (oldest) hunter.
2. Any forager that has not yet eaten; if two or more have not yet eaten, starve the one with the lowest population first; if still tied, starve the leftmost (oldest) species.
3. Any remaining forager, starving leftmost (oldest) first, if more than one remains.

### Losing Population

Rani will always choose a hungry population to be killed, in preference to a fed population. Keep any killed population next to the solo card to keep track during the round.

## Extinction

When one of Rani’s species goes extinct, place the solo card on the bottom of Rani’s deck and follow the extinction rules from the core Nature rulebook.

## Scoring

Before scoring, place any of Rani’s killed population next to her player screen. For each of Rani’s species that had 1 or more population killed this round:

- Switch which priority lists that species uses in the next round. If the species used the priority lists below the blue tree in the last round, cover the priority lists below the blue tree and use the priority lists below the purple tree next round, and vice versa.
- Shuffle that species’ existing trait cards and discard one at random. If that species’ Hunter trait is discarded, they will switch back to being a forager.

The game ends after four rounds. Score more points than Rani to win the game.
