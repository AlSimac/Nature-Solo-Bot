# Nature Core Rulebook

## Overview

Nature is bursting with life.

Earth is home to millions of species — plants, fungi, insects, birds, reptiles, and mammals — each with an innate drive to grow and flourish. The game of Nature simulates a dynamic ecosystem around a small watering hole. The goal is to grow your population while navigating two threats: starvation and predation. Strategically adapt to the changing environment and your species will flourish and thrive, but adapt poorly and your population will dwindle.

There are thousands of unique species to discover. Welcome to the incredible diversity of Nature.

## Components

### Population

The goal of Nature is to have a flourishing population. Population represents animals that need to be fed and protected.

### Food

Every population needs food. There are two types of food: plant and meat. Each food that sustains a population for a round will be worth 1 point at the end of the game.

### Player Screens

Place your food behind your player screen (or in your deluxe player bag) at the end of each round. This keeps the score hidden until the end of the game.

### Watering Hole

The watering hole is the only place for animals to eat plants. A limited number of plants are added each round, so get to the watering hole before the food runs out.

### Hunter Cards

Any player can use a hunter card to turn their species into a hunter. A hunter no longer eats plants from the watering hole. Instead, it eats meat by hunting animals around the watering hole.

### Size Dials

Every species has a size dial to keep track of its size. Larger species eat more food when they feed at the watering hole, and are better defended against hunters.

### Nature Cards

There are 8 different traits in the Nature deck. These traits are powerful abilities that allow your species to adapt to the changing environment as they struggle to survive and thrive.

### Snow Leopard

One of the most awesome first-player markers in evolutionary history.

### Solo Cards

These cards are only used when you play Nature Solo. Each card represents an opponent’s species with a unique personality.

## Setup

1. Place the watering hole on the table.
2. Place the size dials on opposite sides of the table.
3. Place the population next to the size dials.
4. Place the food trays with plant and meat near the watering hole.
5. Shuffle the Nature cards and place them face-down to form the Nature deck.
6. Place the Hunter cards near the watering hole.
7. Give a player screen (or deluxe player bag) to each player.
8. The person who can make the most amazing animal noise goes first. Give them the snow leopard.
9. A set amount of food will be added to the watering hole at the start of each round. Draw the top card of the Nature deck, then multiply the food number shown in the bottom-right corner by the number of players.
10. This is the amount of plants that will be added to the watering hole each round. Tuck the card under the watering hole to remind you how much food to add in future rounds.

## Playing Nature

## Goal

The goal of Nature is to grow your population in an ever-changing ecosystem where food is scarce and predators lurk. You will receive 1 point for every population you keep alive each round. To track this, you will place the food eaten by your population behind your player screen (or in your deluxe player bag) at the end of each round.

At the end of the game, you will score:

- 1 point for every plant and every meat behind your player screens (or in your deluxe player bag).
- 2 points for each of your surviving species as a Game End Bonus.

## Game Structure

Nature is played over four rounds. In each round, go through the following steps:

1. Preparation
2. Add Food to the Watering Hole
3. Get a New Species
4. Draw 5 Cards
5. Adapting Phase
6. Play Cards
7. Free Actions
8. Reveal Traits
9. Feeding Phase
10. Feeding Actions
11. Free Actions
12. Scoring

## Preparation

### 1. Add Plants

Add the number of plants determined during setup to the watering hole. These are in addition to any plants left over from the previous round.

### 2. New Species

Every round, each player takes the following:

- A size dial showing size 1
- A population

The size dial represents the physical size of the animals and the population represents the number of animals that need to be fed each round. Place the size dial forager side up to show that it takes the forage action when feeding.

#### Lost Population

If you lost population in the previous round, add all lost population to your new species. For example, if 3 of your population were killed in the previous round, your new species will start with 4 population.

#### Lost Size

If your species became extinct in the previous round, add the lost size to your new species. For example, if a size 3 species went extinct, your new species will start at size 4. Or if you lost two size 1 species in the previous round, your new species will start at size 3.

Lost size in excess of the largest number on the size dial are lost forever (size 4 in Nature and size 10 with the Jurassic module).

### 3. Draw Cards

Each player draws 5 cards from the Nature deck. There is no maximum hand size, so these 5 cards are in addition to cards a player may have from a previous round.

If you run out of cards in the Nature deck, shuffle the discard pile and make a new Nature draw deck.

## Adapting Phase

The theme of the Adapting Phase takes place over millions of years as species adapt to the changing environment. Each player only gets one turn during this phase, so plan ahead.

### Play Cards

The player with the snow leopard is the first player to take their turn. Each card in your hand can be used for the following actions:

- Increase Population
- Increase Size
- Play a Trait

On your turn, you may play as many cards as you like. Each action can be taken multiple times and in any order.

#### Increase Population

Take any card from your hand and place it face-up in the discard pile, then take a population from the supply and add it to any one of your species.

There is no limit to the number of population a species can have.

#### Increase Size

Take any card from your hand and place it face-up in the discard pile, then increase the size of any one of your species by rotating the size dial to the next number. The maximum size on the Nature size dial is 4, roughly the size of a rhinoceros, moose, or elephant.

The size of a species determines how effective it is at hunting, defending, and foraging. These are explained in the Feeding Phase section.

#### Play a Trait

Take any card from your hand and play it face-down above any one of your species. This card is now a trait that will modify that species once it is flipped face-up. The maximum number of traits a species can have is 3. You may play several of the same traits to a species, but each copy counts towards the 3 trait maximum.

## Free Actions

Free actions allow you to adapt your species without playing a card from your hand. Free actions may be taken multiple times and at any time during your Adapting Phase turn.

### Become a Hunter

You may play a Hunter card as a trait on any of your species. The Hunter trait counts toward the 3 trait limit.

The number of available Hunter cards is unlimited, so use a proxy card if the pile runs out.

When you play a Hunter trait, flip over the size dial of that species to show that it can hunt. It can no longer forage for plants in the watering hole.

### Remove Traits

You may discard traits from any of your species to make room for a different trait. Return Hunter traits to the Hunter deck and place other removed traits in the face-up discard pile. When you remove the Hunter card, flip your size dial back to the forager side. It can no longer hunt other species.

### Hold Cards

You may keep 1 or more cards to play as food during the Feeding Phase, or to play in the next round.

## Reveal Traits

The Adapting Phase ends when every player has taken one turn. Flip all the face-down traits so they are face-up and move into the Feeding Phase.

## About Traits

Traits are played on a species to modify the ability of that species. The rules on a trait card override the core Nature rules. Any combination of up to 3 traits can be played on a forager or a hunter, but 3 traits is the maximum number of traits a species can have.

## Hunt Value and Defense Value

The hunt value and defense value of a species is equal to the number on its size dial, but can be increased by trait cards.

In the top-left of each trait, the value in the red tab adds to the species’ hunt value, while the value in the grey tab adds to the defense value. If these tabs hold no values, nothing is added.

### Trigger Traits

Traits with an exclamation point are trigger traits. They have a mandatory ability that activates when the trigger condition is met. The ability will activate anytime the condition is met, regardless of whose turn it is.

Examples:

- Scavenging triggers anytime a species hunts. It may trigger on your turn or someone else’s turn. When Scavenging triggers, the species eats 2 meat from the supply.
- Opportunistic triggers on your turn if there is plant in the watering hole after one of your species forages or hunts. When Opportunistic triggers, the species eats 2 meat from the supply.
- Tusked triggers anytime the watering hole becomes empty. It may trigger on your turn or someone else’s turn. When Tusked triggers, the species eats 1 meat from the supply.

### Herbivore, Omnivore, and Carnivore

- Herbivore: when a new species enters play at the start of the round, it is a Herbivore. It may eat plants when it forages from the watering hole.
- Omnivore: if a trigger trait is added to the species, it becomes an Omnivore. It may still forage to eat plants from the watering hole, but it will also eat meat if the trigger trait activates.
- Carnivore: if a Hunter trait is added to the species, it becomes a Carnivore. It may no longer forage from the watering hole to eat plants. It hunts to eat meat instead.

## Feeding Phase

The Feeding Phase is a snapshot of the ecosystem at a specific point in time. Each player will get multiple turns during the Feeding Phase to feed their species.

At the start of the Feeding Phase, every population is hungry. Starting with the player who has the snow leopard and moving clockwise around the table, each player will take a feeding turn to feed one of their species.

The Feeding Phase will end when every population is fed or dead:

- fed
- killed by starvation
- killed in a hunt

### Feeding Turns

On your turn, you must take the first possible action out of the following:

1. Take a feeding action with a hungry species.
2. If you cannot take a feeding action, then starve 1 hungry population.
3. If you don’t have any hungry population, you must pass.

The Feeding Phase ends once everyone has passed.

### Forage Feeding Action

A forager may take the forage feeding action as long as it will get food from the action. When a species forages, it eats plants from the watering hole equal to its size, plus any additional amount indicated by its traits.

Place each eaten plant on top of a hungry population.

A species must always eat the full amount unless:

- there are not enough plants in the watering hole.
- your species does not have enough hungry population.

Plant food is limited to the number of plants in the watering hole. If a species can forage for 4, but there are only 2 plants in the watering hole, then it will only eat 2 plants when it forages.

A species may never eat more food than it has hungry population. If your species can forage for 4, but it only has 1 hungry population, then it will only eat 1 plant when it forages, and the remaining 3 will stay in the watering hole.

### Hunt Feeding Action

A species that received a Hunter trait during the Adapting Phase is a hunter.

Hunters can take the hunt feeding action before or after the watering hole runs out of plants.

A hunter may hunt another species if the hunter:

- is hungry
- has a hunt value equal to or greater than the defense value of its prey
- can overcome the defense traits of its prey (such as Fast)

After a successful hunt:

1. The hunter kills 1 population of the hunted species. The player who controls the hunted species chooses which population to flip over. This dead population will get flipped back to the living side and added to their new species at the start of the next round.
2. If the population that was killed had a food token on it, return the food to the supply.
3. The hunter eats meat from the supply equal to the size of the hunted species. Place each eaten meat on top of a hungry population of the hunter. Any extra meat remains in the supply.

A hungry hunter must hunt if there is available prey, regardless of who controls that species. For example, your hunter must hunt a species with the Horned trait, even if that species belongs to you.

### Free Actions During Feeding

You may discard one or more cards from your hand to add plants to the watering hole. Add plants equal to the food value(s) indicated in the bottom-right corner of the discarded card(s).

## Extinction

A species goes extinct when its last population is killed or starves. Do the following if one of your species goes extinct:

- Place its killed population and its size dial next to your player screen. They will be added to your new species next round.
- If the species had a Hunter trait, return it to the Hunter deck.
- Return its other trait(s) to your hand. These cards are available immediately, which means you can discard them to add food to the watering hole during the current Feeding Phase.

Reminder: there is no maximum hand size. You will get 5 additional cards during Preparation in the next round.

## Scoring

Remove the plant and meat from your fed population and place them behind your player screen. They will each be worth 1 point at the end of the game.

If there are plants remaining in the watering hole, leave them there. You will add additional plants next round during Preparation.

The game ends after Round 4. If this is the end of Round 4, proceed to the Game End section. Otherwise, pass the awesome snow leopard to the left and begin a new round, starting with Preparation.

## Game End

To determine the success of your species in Nature, each player scores:

- 1 point for every plant and every meat behind their player screen (or in their deluxe player bag).
- 2 points as a Game End Bonus for each surviving species.

The player with the most points wins.

If there is a tie, the player with the most traits on their surviving species wins. If there is still a tie, order two pizzas — one vegetarian and one meat lovers — and play again.

## Nature Game System

The core game of Nature is a stand-alone experience with a surprising amount of strategic depth. The Nature game system allows you to alter that experience by adding one or more modules. We recommend playing each module on its own before mixing them together. You can add one Location module to change the biome of the ecosystem, along with as many other modules as you like. When you add one or more modules to Nature, play five rounds instead of four. Every combination creates a unique environment to discover.

Each Nature module is designed for a unique style of play. A module might add bluffing or dramatic events, favor aggressive strategies, or reward players who avoid conflict. You can learn more about the Nature modules at NorthStarGames.com.

## Variable Length

You can play additional rounds to Nature if you’d like a longer game. Each additional round adds about 20 minutes to the game. Adding several modules and 2 rounds turns Nature into an epic 2 hour game.

## Quick Play Variant

In this variant, everyone plays their cards during the Adapting Phase simultaneously, without looking at what actions their opponents are taking. This variant can be chaotic, but it more closely represents how random mutations drive evolution through natural selection.

## 5-6 Player Rules

You can play with up to 6 players if you have the Nature Big Box edition (or two copies of Nature). Play with the Quick Play Variant to minimize downtime.

## FAQ

### What happens if we run out of a component?

Components are unlimited. Use something in its place such as a coin, a die, or a plastic miniature you never use from an expensive Kickstarter campaign.

### Can I choose to eat less food than the written amount?

No, but sometimes your species will not eat the full amount because it does not have enough hungry population.

### Can I add the lost and size from the previous round to any species?

No, they must be added to your new species.

### Do I get anything for the card I lose if I discard a trait from my species?

No.

### During the Feeding Phase, can I discard traits from my species to add food to the watering hole?

No, a discarded card must come from your hand.

### If my size 4 species goes extinct, does my new species come in the next round at size 5?

No. The maximum size is indicated on the size dial. In Nature, the largest number on the size dial is 4.

### Who chooses which population is killed when a species is hunted?

The player controlling the species that lost a population chooses which population is killed. It may be a hungry population or a fed population.

### Can I hold onto cards I kept in my hand from one round to the next?

Yes. Any saved cards are added to the 5 cards you draw at the beginning of the next round. There is no limit to your hand size.

### What happens when I remove a trait that turned my species into a hunter?

Flip over the size dial. The species is now a forager. It cannot hunt.

### Is a triggered ability optional?

No. The ability happens every time the trigger condition is met. The word “may” is needed to make something optional.

### Can I look through the discard pile?

No, you can only look at the top card of the discard pile. When someone plays cards to the discard, they play them 1 at a time, giving everyone a chance to see which cards were discarded.

## The History of Nature

Our story begins with Darwin’s revolutionary theory of evolution: that random mutations shaped by natural selection over millions of years, have led to the incredible diversity of life on Earth. Inspired by this concept, Russian biologist Dmitry Knorre created a scientific game as a gift for friends, which eventually became Evolution: The Origin of Species.

Dmitry’s game served as the foundation for the international award-winning hit Evolution, which gained recognition from Nature, the world’s leading scientific journal. The Nature article sparked widespread interest, with other scientific journals highlighting the game and hundreds of universities and schools adopting it as a teaching tool. It was a dream come true to see Evolution bridging science and game design, but its widespread use also revealed opportunities for improvement.

With over a decade of experience designing the Evolution series, Dominic Crapuchettes completely reimagined the game from the ground up. The result is Nature — a streamlined, engaging, and endlessly expandable system that allows players to craft and discover limitless ecosystems.

# Nature Solo Rulebook

## Introduction

In Nature Solo, you play against an automated opponent named Rani ("rah-nee") who uses a deck of solo cards to make decisions. Nature Solo can be played at five different difficulty levels.

A game of Nature Solo is played using the standard Nature rules. Additionally, Rani has special rules that take precedence if there is a conflict. Can you adapt better than Rani?

## Components

### Solo Cards

Each solo card includes:

- a species name
- instructions
- a set icon
- a solo icon

The solo card system defines how Rani chooses which species to assign cards to, when to grow population, when to increase size, and how to behave during feeding and starvation.

## Setup

Nature Solo is set up like a 2-player game of Nature. Follow the steps in the Nature rulebook, then do the following:

- Choose a difficulty: Mouse (easiest), Rabbit, Fox (medium), Tiger, or Snow Leopard (most challenging). If it is your first game, we recommend playing at the Mouse difficulty.
- Set up a player screen for Rani.
- Shuffle and place the six solo cards face-down to create Rani’s deck.
- Choose who will play first between you and Rani. There will be four rounds.

## Preparation

### New Species

When getting a new species for Rani, use the standard rules with the following changes:

- When Rani gets a new species, it is always placed to the right of any existing species in her player area.
- Each of Rani’s species will have a solo card. Draw the top card of Rani’s deck and play it face-up with the new species. This is not a trait card, so it does not count toward the limit of 3 traits.
- Whenever drawing a new solo card, cover the icons below the blue tree (the right-hand side) using the size dial, or any component you prefer. Pay attention to any species instructions, if applicable.

### First Round Only

Rani starts the game with an additional species. After creating Rani’s first species, immediately make a second species for Rani. Draw a solo card for each species.

The second species starts with population equal to the number in your selected difficulty.

### Draw Cards

Rani draws cards equal to the number in your selected difficulty.

Do not look at Rani’s cards. Place them face-down in a stack, hereafter called her “hand.” If Rani has any cards set aside from a previous round, shuffle those into her hand after drawing new cards.

## Adapting Phase

You always go first during the Adapting Phase.

The Adapting Phase uses the standard rules with the following changes for Rani:

### Fourth Round Only

Set aside one card from Rani’s hand for each of Rani’s species that can forage from the watering hole. Keep these cards face-down and set aside until step 3, when they are considered back in her hand.

### Play Rani’s Hand

Draw a card from the top of Rani’s hand one at a time. Follow these steps for each card drawn:

1. Check the solo icon in the bottom-left corner of the drawn card to determine species priority.
2. Assign this card to Rani’s species with the solo icon highest on its priority list. Whenever two or more species have the solo icon at the same priority, assign it to the leftmost (oldest) species first.
3. Each solo icon on the priority list has three unique presentations that determine the action taken:
   - No border: the card is added to the species as a trait.
   - In a circle: discard the card and increase population.
   - In a D-shaped border: discard the card and increase size.
   - If the species is already at maximum size, skip this species and assign the drawn card to the species with this solo icon in the next highest priority.
4. The solo icon may also be red, which requires an additional step:
   - Red Icon: give this species the Hunter trait if it is not a hunter.
   - If this species already has three traits, shuffle its existing trait cards and randomly discard one before adding the Hunter trait.
5. If no species can be assigned the card, Rani will keep the card face-down in her hand.

### Special Rule: No Foraging Species

If Rani has no species that can forage from the watering hole in the next phase, then follow the additional Adapting Phase rules below:

- Rani will use all cards in her face-down hand from step 2 to increase size for her species. For each card, Rani will increase the size of the species that has the card’s solo icon at the highest priority.
- If that species is at maximum size, move on to the next species with the card’s solo icon at the highest priority.
- If priority is tied, the card goes to the leftmost (oldest) species first.
- Once all of Rani’s species are at maximum size, she will use any remaining cards to increase population for the species with the card’s solo icon at the highest priority.
- If priority is tied, the card goes to the leftmost (oldest) species first.

## Rani’s Feeding Phase

The Feeding Phase uses the standard rules with the following changes for Rani:

1. Rani will always forage first if she can. Rani will first eat with the species that will provide her with the most food by foraging. If two or more are tied, she will eat with the leftmost (oldest) species.
2. If Rani cannot take the forage action, she will hunt, starting with the leftmost (oldest) species that can hunt.

### Foraging Species

Rani will choose which forager to eat based on plant availability in the watering hole.

When determining how much food can be eaten, Rani will consider there to be three additional plants in the watering hole for each card left in her hand. For example, if Rani has two cards in her hand and one plant remaining in the watering hole, she will consider there to be seven plants in the watering hole: six from the two cards she has in her hand plus one visible in the watering hole.

Before the forager eats, if there is less food in the watering hole than the forager can eat, Rani will discard a random card from her hand and add plants to the watering hole equal to the food value printed on the discarded card. She will do this multiple times if needed until there is as much plant as the forager can eat.

### Hunting Species

Rani will choose which species to feed when hunting, in this order:

1. She will eat the species that loses her the least population across all her species.
2. She will prefer to eat one of your species before her own.
3. If there are ties, she will try to eat the smallest (size dial only) of those species first.
4. If still tied, she will try to eat the leftmost (oldest) of those species first.

### Starving

If Rani has hungry population but cannot eat, Rani will starve population in this order:

1. The leftmost (oldest) hunter.
2. Any forager that has not yet eaten; if two or more have not yet eaten, starve the one with the lowest population first; if still tied, starve the leftmost (oldest) species.
3. Any remaining forager, starving leftmost (oldest) first, if more than one remains.

### Losing Population

Rani will always choose a hungry population to be killed, in preference to a fed population. Keep any killed population next to the solo card to keep track during the round.

## Extinction

When one of Rani’s species goes extinct, place the solo card on the bottom of Rani’s deck and follow the extinction rules from the core Nature rulebook.

## Scoring

Before scoring, place any of Rani’s killed population next to her player screen. For each of Rani’s species that had 1 or more population killed this round:

- switch which priority lists that species uses in the next round. If the species used the priority lists below the blue tree in the last round, cover the priority lists below the blue tree and use the priority lists below the purple tree next round, and vice versa.
- shuffle that species’ existing trait cards and discard one at random. If that species’ Hunter trait is discarded, they will switch back to being a forager.

The game ends after four rounds. Score more points than Rani to win the game.

# Nature Reference Guide

The Nature reference guide provides additional detail on key words, trait cards, round structure, and iconography.

## Key Words

The key words have specific in-game meanings that explain how a trait will interact with the core rules and with other traits.

### Feeding Action

There are two basic feeding actions: foraging and hunting.

- Forage: when a species forages, it eats plant(s) from the watering hole equal to the number on its size dial.
- Hunt: when a species hunts, it eats meat from the supply equal to the size of the hunted species, and 1 population of the hunted species is killed.
- Starve: when a species starves, choose a hungry population of that species to be killed.
- Pass: once a player passes as their feeding action, they are out of the Feeding Phase for the remainder of the round.

### Other Actions

- Eats: a species may eat only if it has hungry population. When a species eats, place each eaten food on one of its hungry population.
- Killed: when a population is killed, flip it over so this side is showing and return any food on it to the supply. Killed population will be added as population to your new species in the next round. The player who controls the population that is killed chooses which population is killed.

### Other Key Words

- Card: a card is either in the draw deck, the discard pile, or your hand. Cards played on species are called traits. Discarding a card from your hand provides a benefit. Discarding a trait does not provide a benefit.
- Extinction: a species goes extinct when its last population is killed or starves.
- Food: plant and meat are food used to keep population from starving.
- Fed Population: a fed population has food on it.
- Fully Fed: a species is fully fed when none of its population are hungry.
- Hungry: a population is hungry when it doesn’t have food on it. A species is hungry when it has at least 1 hungry population.
- Ignores: a species that ignores a defensive trait while hunting acts as if that trait does not exist. This keyword is used in several modules.
- Size: every species has a size dial to indicate its size. The size of a species determines how effective it is at hunting, defending, and foraging.
- Species: a species consists of a size dial, at least 1 population, and between 0 and 3 traits.
- Trait: when a card is played on a species, it is called a trait. Each species may have up to 3 traits. Any trait that is removed from a species becomes a card again.

Each trait in the Nature deck has effects that are additive, so having two copies of the same trait will have double the effect, and having three copies will have triple the effect.

## Trait Cards

### Clawed

A species with Clawed adds 2 to their hunt value when hunting, and 1 to their defense value when being hunted.

When eating plants, a species with Clawed will eat 2 additional plants from the same source.

### Fast

A species with Fast cannot be hunted by a species with fewer copies of the Fast trait.

For example, a hunter with 1 copy of Fast can hunt a species with 1 copy of Fast, but cannot hunt a species with 2 or 3 copies of Fast.

### Horned

When a species with Horned is hunted, it will kill population of the hunter. The number of population it kills will be equal to its size.

For example, when a size 1 species with Horned is hunted, it will kill 1 population of the hunter, and when a size 3 species with Horned is hunted, it will kill 3 population of the hunter.

When a species with Horned is hunted, 1 of its population will be killed as normal, even if the hunter goes extinct.

### Hunter

When the Hunter trait is played, flip its size dial to the HUNTER side. A species with Hunter can no longer take the forage feeding action. It takes the hunt feeding action instead.

A hunter can hunt a species if:

- its hunt value is equal to or greater than the defense value of the hunted species,
- it has the traits necessary to overcome the hunted species’ defensive traits,
- it has hungry population.

### Opportunistic

A species with Opportunistic adds 1 to their defense value when being hunted.

Opportunistic triggers after any of your species forages or hunts, as long as there are plants in the watering hole after the feeding action is taken. When Opportunistic triggers, the species with Opportunistic eats 2 meat from the supply.

Each Opportunistic triggers independently. For example, if a species has 2 copies of Opportunistic and a Social, it would eat a total of 8 meat.

### Plated

A species with Plated adds 4 to their defense value when being hunted.

### Scavenging

Scavenging triggers after any species hunts. This may happen on your feeding turn or on someone else’s feeding turn.

When Scavenging triggers, the species with Scavenging eats 2 meat from the supply.

Each Scavenging triggers independently. For example, if a species has 2 copies of Scavenging and a Social, it would eat a total of 8 meat.

### Social

A species with Social gets 2 additional food when it eats. The additional food comes from the same source as the original food: from the watering hole when it’s plant(s), and from the supply when it’s meat.

Social works when a species forages or hunts, but it can be combined with any trait that eats, such as Opportunistic, Scavenging, and Tusked.

### Tusked

A species with Tusked adds 2 to their defense value when being hunted.

Tusked triggers any time the last plant is removed from the watering hole. Tusked traits trigger regardless of who the current player is and may trigger multiple times in a round if additional food is added to the watering hole.

When Tusked triggers, the species with Tusked eats 1 meat from the supply.

Each Tusked triggers independently. For example, if a species has 2 copies of Tusked and a Social, it would eat a total of 6 meat.

## Round Structure

This chart shows the round structure in Nature combined with additional steps from Modules 1-6.

### Preparation

1. Roll the climate die
2. Add plants to the watering hole
3. Add fed population to the faraway place
4. Get a new species
5. Draw cards

### Adapting Phase

1. Play cards
2. Reveal trait cards

### Feeding Phase

1. Move Fat Tissue food
2. Take feeding turns
3. Resolve climate

### Scoring

1. Migrated species return
2. Nesting triggers
3. Put food behind player screens
4. Game End Bonus
5. Flip Disaster Card
